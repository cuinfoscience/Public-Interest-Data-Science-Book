18  Public Comments

Counties decide what gets built where, and almost nobody you know has written them a comment about it. In unincorporated Boulder County, the Community Planning and Permitting department reviews land use applications under the Boulder County Comprehensive Plan and the county’s Land Use Code. A nine-member Planning Commission, appointed by the Board of County Commissioners, holds public hearings on many of those applications and on proposed changes to the code itself. For a few matters (the Comprehensive Plan and Location and Extent review) the Planning Commission decides; for everything else it recommends, and the Board of County Commissioners makes the binding decision at a later public hearing (Boulder County Land Use Department 2014; Boulder County, n.d.). At every step, anyone may comment in writing, cite the docket number, and have the comment made part of the public record. The door is open. Most of the profession walks past it.

A public comment is a written argument submitted to a body that must consider it before deciding, on that body’s schedule. It is the planning lineage’s genre. Planning (Chapter 9) asks who was in the room and whose knowledge counts as evidence; the comment period is its standing answer, a room anyone may enter, in writing, before a deadline. This chapter works at the county, where land use decisions are made and recorded. You will find a live docket in the county’s own data, read the referral for the criteria the decision turns on, compute evidence from county open data, and draft a comment that carries it. A final section takes the genre to the state and federal levels, where agencies writing rules must take written comment too.

18.1 How a county land use docket works

Every application or code amendment gets a docket number. The prefix names the type and the middle digits the year: SU-25-0002 is a Special Use Review docket from 2025, DC-25-0003 a Land Use Code text amendment. Staff refer the docket to other agencies and to the public, collect comments, and write a staff report that evaluates the proposal against the criteria in the Land Use Code. The staff report is the document your comment has to answer, because it is the document the commissioners will read.

Timing matters more than at any other level of government. The county’s referral memo for docket DC-25-0003, a 2025 package of Land Use Code clarifications, told the public that comments received by a stated deadline would go into the Planning Commission’s staff packet, and that later comments would be shared with the commission and included in the packet for the Board of County Commissioners’ hearing. It also said, plainly, “All comments will be made part of the public record” (Boulder County Community Planning and Permitting 2025). The county’s published guidelines for Planning Commission hearings add three practical rules: public testimony is generally limited to three minutes per speaker, speakers present together may pool time up to ten minutes, and handouts or long documents given to staff at least eight days before the hearing can be included in the commissioners’ packets. They also say that the official record of a hearing is its audio recording (Boulder County Land Use Department 2014).

County comments are written, which suits data scientists. They are read, because they go into the commissioners’ packet. And they enter a record that outlasts the decision: years later, when someone asks why a parcel was rezoned, the docket file is where they look.

18.2 Why data scientists underuse this genre

Comments are lower-friction than testimony and higher-leverage than op-eds, and the profession submits shockingly few. Nobody invited you; the form is unfamiliar, since no county publishes a style guide for comments the way newspapers publish pitch guidelines; and the outcome is uncertain, because the commission may adopt your proposed condition, mention your comment in passing, or neither. A genre whose effect is cumulative feels unrewarding to a profession that likes metrics. It is, nonetheless, how much of the built environment gets shaped.

18.3 The substantive comment standard

Decision makers distinguish substantive comments from expressions of sentiment. A substantive comment identifies the specific provisions or criteria it responds to, offers evidence or analysis the staff report does not already contain, and proposes specific changes: a revised code sentence, a condition of approval, a request that staff supply a missing analysis before the hearing. A sentiment comment says the writer supports or opposes the proposal. A hundred letters of opposition tell the commissioners how a neighborhood feels; one substantive comment can change what the staff report says.

County land use has a wrinkle the federal process lacks. Decisions on individual applications are often treated as quasi-judicial: the commissioners act like judges applying the code’s criteria to evidence in the record, and contacts with them outside the hearing may be restricted. Code amendments, by contrast, are legislative. Ask the docket planner which kind yours is. For a quasi-judicial docket, put everything in the written record and tie every claim to a criterion.

TipThe Missing Manual

Boulder County publishes its land use dockets as a public map layer, and the layer behaves like what it is: a view of a permitting database, not a dataset designed for you. Old docket numbers reappear with new application dates (a 1985 docket can show a 2026 date when the record is reopened), many records have no status, and the status vocabulary is long and inconsistent (“Closed,” “Closed Complete,” “Accepted Completed”). The server returns at most 1,000 records per request, so a broad query silently stops at 1,000 unless you page with resultOffset. The layer also carries owner names. You do not need them to write a comment, so do not request them: name the fields you want in outFields rather than asking for *. Finally, the county’s zoning layer includes each incorporated municipality as a single polygon with an X prefix (XBO for Boulder); county zoning does not apply there, so drop those polygons before computing anything about the unincorporated county.

18.4 Tutorial, part 1: finding a live docket

Community Planning and Permitting lists applications under review on its website and links them to a map. The same records are queryable through the county’s ArcGIS REST service, which means you can filter dockets by type and date without scraping.

import requests
import pandas as pd

ACCELA = ("https://maps.bouldercounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/"
          "PLANNING/OP_Accela_Poly/MapServer/0/query")
FIELDS = "CAPID,DocketType,ApplicationDate,ApplicationStatus,DocumentTitle,DocketLink"
HEARD = ["Special Use Review", "Rezoning", "Location and Extent Review",
         "Boulder County Comprehensive Plan Amendment"]

def dockets(since="2025-01-01"):
    types = ", ".join(f"'{t}'" for t in HEARD)
    params = {"where": f"DocketType IN ({types}) AND ApplicationDate >= DATE '{since}'",
              "outFields": FIELDS, "returnGeometry": "false",
              "orderByFields": "ApplicationDate DESC", "f": "json"}
    r = requests.get(ACCELA, params=params, timeout=60)
    r.raise_for_status()
    df = pd.DataFrame([f["attributes"] for f in r.json()["features"]])
    df["ApplicationDate"] = pd.to_datetime(df["ApplicationDate"], unit="ms").dt.date
    return df

d = dockets()
print(len(d))
print(d[["CAPID", "ApplicationDate", "ApplicationStatus"]].head(4).to_string(index=False))
# => 12
# =>      CAPID ApplicationDate ApplicationStatus
# => SU-26-0003      2026-04-28        Incomplete
# => SU-26-0002      2026-03-10        Incomplete
# => SU-85-0023      2026-03-05              None
# => LE-90-0008      2026-02-25              None
# (as of October 2026; results change as dockets move)

The HEARD list picks docket types that usually go to a public hearing. Each DocketLink opens the docket page, with the referral packet, staff report, and hearing dates. Code amendment (DC) dockets are listed separately on the county’s Land Use Code update page.

18.5 Tutorial, part 2: evidence for a code amendment

Take DC-25-0003 as a worked example. Its referral packet proposed, among other things, new text for parking standards in three zoning districts: Transitional (T), Multiple Family (MF), and Niwot Rural Community District I (NRCD I) (Boulder County Community Planning and Permitting 2025). Its referral period closed in December 2025, so treat it as practice. A reader of the packet would want to know a simple thing the packet does not say: how much of the county does the parking change touch? The county’s open zoning and parcel layers answer it.

import geopandas as gpd

BASE = "https://maps.bouldercounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/"
ZONING = BASE + "PLANNING/LUC_ZoningDistricts/MapServer/0/query"
PARCELS = BASE + "Emap/BOCO_Parcels/MapServer/0/query"

def layer(url, **params):
    params = {"outSR": 4326, "f": "geojson", **params}
    r = requests.get(url, params=params, timeout=60)
    r.raise_for_status()
    return gpd.GeoDataFrame.from_features(r.json()["features"], crs="EPSG:4326")

zones = layer(ZONING, where="ZONECLASS IN ('T','MF','NRCD1')",
              outFields="ZONECLASS,ZONEDESC").to_crs("EPSG:26913")
zones["acres"] = zones.area / 4046.8564224          # UTM 13N, meters
print(zones.groupby("ZONECLASS").agg(polygons=("acres", "size"),
                                     acres=("acres", "sum")).round(1))
# =>            polygons  acres
# => ZONECLASS
# => MF                7  234.6
# => NRCD1             1   10.1
# => T                 7   50.4

About 295 acres, roughly 0.07 percent of the unincorporated county’s zoned area. The parcel count tells a different story:

frames = []
for geom in zones.to_crs(4326).geometry:   # one box per polygon; each stays under the 5,000-record cap
    x0, y0, x1, y1 = geom.bounds
    frames.append(layer(PARCELS, where="1=1", outFields="PARCEL_NO",
                        geometry=f"{x0},{y0},{x1},{y1}",
                        geometryType="esriGeometryEnvelope", inSR=4326))
parcels = pd.concat(frames).drop_duplicates("PARCEL_NO").to_crs("EPSG:26913")
parcels["geometry"] = parcels.representative_point()
inside = gpd.sjoin(parcels, zones[["ZONECLASS", "geometry"]], predicate="within")
print(inside.groupby("ZONECLASS")["PARCEL_NO"].nunique().to_dict())
# => {'MF': 1626, 'NRCD1': 27, 'T': 155}

A tiny share of the land, but about 1,800 parcels, most in multifamily districts where condominium units are often separate parcels. That finding neither supports nor opposes the amendment, but it tells the commission whom the amendment affects, and it suggests a request (map the districts in the packet; notify the affected owners and residents) the county could grant without changing a word of the proposal.

18.6 The shape of a substantive county comment

A county comment has a header, an introduction, a body organized by the criteria or code sections at issue, and a conclusion listing specific requests.

[Name, address or affiliation, contact]          [Date]

To: Community Planning and Permitting, [docket planner]
Re: Docket [DC-25-0003], [title]
    Comment for the [Planning Commission / Board of County Commissioners] packet

The introduction says who you are, which sections of the proposal you address, and your position in a paragraph. The body answers the criteria:

Proposed parking standards for the T, MF, and NRCD I districts (Appendix A)

The referral packet does not state how many properties the change affects.
Using the county's zoning and parcel layers (Exhibit A, with code), I
count about 1,800 parcels within these districts, 1,626 of them in MF
districts. I ask that the staff report include a map of the affected
districts and that notice of the hearing reach owners and residents of
these parcels.

That paragraph names the section, adds evidence the packet lacked, shows its method, and makes a request staff could adopt as written. Attach the exhibits (the CSV, the map, the notebook), send it before the packet deadline, and if you can, speak at the hearing, pointing to the written comment rather than reading it.

18.7 Tracking the docket

A docket accumulates documents over months: referral, staff report, recommendation, resolution. The SQLite tracker for records requests in Chapter 7 extends naturally with one table that serves every level of government.

import sqlite3

SCHEMA = """
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS dockets (
    docket_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,           -- 'DC-25-0003', 'FTC-2022-0053'
    level TEXT CHECK (level IN ('county','state','federal')),
    body TEXT NOT NULL, title TEXT NOT NULL,
    comment_deadline TEXT, hearing_date TEXT,
    status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('open','closed','decided','withdrawn')),
    my_comment_url TEXT);
"""
with sqlite3.connect("docket_tracker.sqlite") as conn:
    conn.executescript(SCHEMA)

A weekly job that flags dockets whose comment deadline falls within fourteen days will keep you from the most common first-time failure: missing the packet.

NoteIn the Public Interest

A county comment is ownership in procedural form. Chapter 5 defines ownership as collective, accountable governance over how public-relevant data and decisions are kept and used, and Chapter 9 shows how planning gave participation procedural teeth. A comment that enters the docket file is the public asserting a stake in a decision it does not control, in a record the county must keep. It is also oversight at the smallest scale, because your comment is now in the record the decision must rest on. The specific claim of this chapter is that a comment advances these values only when it adds checkable evidence about the criteria the decision turns on. A comment that reads as sentiment is participation of the kind Arnstein (1969) placed on the lower rungs: heard, counted, and set aside. Write it so it reads well out of context years from now, because that is when someone opening the docket file may need it.

18.8 Commenting at the state and federal levels

The genre works the same way above the county, with longer documents and more formal rules. In Colorado, the State Administrative Procedure Act requires an agency proposing a rule to publish notice in the Colorado Register and give the public an opportunity to submit written comments and to be heard. Check the Register when a technology law you care about passes; rulemaking is where it becomes operational.

At the federal level, the Administrative Procedure Act’s rulemaking section, 5 U.S.C. § 553, requires an agency proposing a substantive rule to publish notice in the Federal Register, give the public an opportunity to participate “through submission of written data, views, or arguments,” and consider what it receives (US Congress 1946). Substantive comments become part of the administrative record a court examines if the rule is challenged, and courts look for an agency’s engagement with substantive comments, not a response to each copy of a campaign letter (Congressional Research Service 2023). Agencies treat identical comments as one comment with many signatories, and the Federal Communications Commission’s 2017 net neutrality docket showed that floods can be faked: millions of its comments were filed under fabricated or stolen identities. In 2025 the White House directed agencies to lean on the “good cause” exception when repealing rules, which skips comment altogether.

For data scientists, the most useful federal dockets are often Paperwork Reduction Act notices. An agency that wants to start, change, or renew a collection of information from the public (a survey, a reporting form, the required fields of a federal data system) must publish a notice and invite comment, first for 60 days and then for 30 while the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs reviews it. The notices ask the same four questions every time: whether the collection is necessary and has practical utility, whether the burden estimate is accurate, how to enhance the quality, utility, and clarity of the information, and how to minimize the burden on respondents. “Practical utility” is where an erosion audit (Chapter 5) shows who depends on a dataset an agency proposes to cut; “minimize the burden” is where a refusal specification (Chapter 13) argues a field should not be collected at all.

Regulations.gov, the shared federal docket system, has an API through api.data.gov with a free key (US General Services Administration 2024). Document search, docket retrieval, and comment retrieval are separate endpoints; submission uses a separate key, and for a single comment the web form is simpler.

import os

API_KEY = os.environ["REGULATIONS_GOV_API_KEY"]
BASE = "https://api.regulations.gov/v4"
HEADERS = {"X-Api-Key": API_KEY}

def open_documents(keyword, doc_type="Proposed Rule", after="2027-04-01"):
    params = {"filter[searchTerm]": keyword,
              "filter[documentType]": doc_type,      # or "Notice" for PRA notices
              "filter[commentEndDate][ge]": after,    # still open on this date
              "page[size]": 25, "sort": "-postedDate"}
    r = requests.get(f"{BASE}/documents", headers=HEADERS, params=params, timeout=30)
    r.raise_for_status()
    return r.json()["data"]

for h in open_documents("information collection", doc_type="Notice")[:5]:
    a = h["attributes"]
    print(a["docketId"], a.get("commentEndDate"), a["title"][:60])
# (results depend on the day you run it)

The API has changed versions and authentication more than once; confirm the current version before writing code. A federal comment has the same shape as a county one, with the Federal Register citation in the header and the body organized by the notice’s numbered questions. The Federal Trade Commission’s 2022 commercial surveillance docket (FTC-2022-0053) holds good academic comments to read as templates.

## Tutorial, part 1: finding a live docket

Community Planning and Permitting lists applications under review on its website and links them to a map. The same records are queryable through the county's ArcGIS REST service, which means you can filter dockets by type and date without scraping.

```python
import requests
import pandas as pd

ACCELA = ("https://maps.bouldercounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/"
          "PLANNING/OP_Accela_Poly/MapServer/0/query")
FIELDS = "CAPID,DocketType,ApplicationDate,ApplicationStatus,DocumentTitle,DocketLink"
HEARD = ["Special Use Review", "Rezoning", "Location and Extent Review",
         "Boulder County Comprehensive Plan Amendment"]

def dockets(since="2025-01-01"):
    types = ", ".join(f"'{t}'" for t in HEARD)
    params = {"where": f"DocketType IN ({types}) AND ApplicationDate >= DATE '{since}'",
              "outFields": FIELDS, "returnGeometry": "false",
              "orderByFields": "ApplicationDate DESC", "f": "json"}
    r = requests.get(ACCELA, params=params, timeout=60)
    r.raise_for_status()
    df = pd.DataFrame([f["attributes"] for f in r.json()["features"]])
    df["ApplicationDate"] = pd.to_datetime(df["ApplicationDate"], unit="ms").dt.date
    return df

d = dockets()
print(len(d))
print(d[["CAPID", "ApplicationDate", "ApplicationStatus"]].head(4).to_string(index=False))
# => 12
# =>      CAPID ApplicationDate ApplicationStatus
# => SU-26-0003      2026-04-28        Incomplete
# => SU-26-0002      2026-03-10        Incomplete
# => SU-85-0023      2026-03-05              None
# => LE-90-0008      2026-02-25              None

```

The `HEARD` list picks docket types that usually go to a public hearing. Each `DocketLink` opens the docket page, with the referral packet, staff report, and hearing dates. Code amendment (`DC`) dockets are listed separately on the county's Land Use Code update page.

## Tutorial, part 2: evidence for a code amendment

Take DC-25-0003 as a worked example. Its referral packet proposed, among other things, new text for parking standards in three zoning districts: Transitional (T), Multiple Family (MF), and Niwot Rural Community District I (NRCD I) [@bouldercounty_dc250003]. Its referral period closed in December 2025, so treat it as practice. <!-- TODO: check the docket's final disposition (hearing dates and whether the Board adopted the amendments) before describing its outcome. --> A reader of the packet would want to know a simple thing the packet does not say: how much of the county does the parking change touch? The county's open zoning and parcel layers answer it.

```python
import geopandas as gpd

BASE = "https://maps.bouldercounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/"
ZONING = BASE + "PLANNING/LUC_ZoningDistricts/MapServer/0/query"
PARCELS = BASE + "Emap/BOCO_Parcels/MapServer/0/query"

def layer(url, **params):
    params = {"outSR": 4326, "f": "geojson", **params}
    r = requests.get(url, params=params, timeout=60)
    r.raise_for_status()
    return gpd.GeoDataFrame.from_features(r.json()["features"], crs="EPSG:4326")

zones = layer(ZONING, where="ZONECLASS IN ('T','MF','NRCD1')",
              outFields="ZONECLASS,ZONEDESC").to_crs("EPSG:26913")
zones["acres"] = zones.area / 4046.8564224          # UTM 13N, meters
print(zones.groupby("ZONECLASS").agg(polygons=("acres", "size"),
                                     acres=("acres", "sum")).round(1))
# =>            polygons  acres
# => ZONECLASS
# => MF                7  234.6
# => NRCD1             1   10.1
# => T                 7   50.4
```

About 295 acres, roughly 0.07 percent of the unincorporated county's zoned area. The parcel count tells a different story:

```python
frames = []
for geom in zones.to_crs(4326).geometry:   # one box per polygon; each stays under the 5,000-record cap
    x0, y0, x1, y1 = geom.bounds
    frames.append(layer(PARCELS, where="1=1", outFields="PARCEL_NO",
                        geometry=f"{x0},{y0},{x1},{y1}",
                        geometryType="esriGeometryEnvelope", inSR=4326))
parcels = pd.concat(frames).drop_duplicates("PARCEL_NO").to_crs("EPSG:26913")
parcels["geometry"] = parcels.representative_point()
inside = gpd.sjoin(parcels, zones[["ZONECLASS", "geometry"]], predicate="within")
print(inside.groupby("ZONECLASS")["PARCEL_NO"].nunique().to_dict())
# => {'MF': 1626, 'NRCD1': 27, 'T': 155}
```

A tiny share of the land, but about 1,800 parcels, most in multifamily districts where condominium units are often separate parcels. That finding neither supports nor opposes the amendment, but it tells the commission whom the amendment affects, and it suggests a request (map the districts in the packet; notify the affected owners and residents) the county could grant without changing a word of the proposal.

## The shape of a substantive county comment

A county comment has a header, an introduction, a body organized by the criteria or code sections at issue, and a conclusion listing specific requests.

```
[Name, address or affiliation, contact]          [Date]

To: Community Planning and Permitting, [docket planner]
Re: Docket [DC-25-0003], [title]
    Comment for the [Planning Commission / Board of County Commissioners] packet
```

The introduction says who you are, which sections of the proposal you address, and your position in a paragraph. The body answers the criteria:

```
Proposed parking standards for the T, MF, and NRCD I districts (Appendix A)

The referral packet does not state how many properties the change affects.
Using the county's zoning and parcel layers (Exhibit A, with code), I
count about 1,800 parcels within these districts, 1,626 of them in MF
districts. I ask that the staff report include a map of the affected
districts and that notice of the hearing reach owners and residents of
these parcels.
```

That paragraph names the section, adds evidence the packet lacked, shows its method, and makes a request staff could adopt as written. Attach the exhibits (the CSV, the map, the notebook), send it before the packet deadline, and if you can, speak at the hearing, pointing to the written comment rather than reading it.

## Tracking the docket

A docket accumulates documents over months: referral, staff report, recommendation, resolution. The SQLite tracker for records requests in @sec-ch-07-law extends naturally with one table that serves every level of government.

```python
import sqlite3

SCHEMA = """
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS dockets (
    docket_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,           -- 'DC-25-0003', 'FTC-2022-0053'
    level TEXT CHECK (level IN ('county','state','federal')),
    body TEXT NOT NULL, title TEXT NOT NULL,
    comment_deadline TEXT, hearing_date TEXT,
    status TEXT CHECK (status IN ('open','closed','decided','withdrawn')),
    my_comment_url TEXT);
"""
with sqlite3.connect("docket_tracker.sqlite") as conn:
    conn.executescript(SCHEMA)
```

A weekly job that flags dockets whose comment deadline falls within fourteen days will keep you from the most common first-time failure: missing the packet.

::: {.callout-note title="In the Public Interest"}
A county comment is **ownership** in procedural form. @sec-ch-05-erosion-ownership defines ownership as collective, accountable governance over how public-relevant data and decisions are kept and used, and @sec-ch-09-planning shows how planning gave participation procedural teeth. A comment that enters the docket file is the public asserting a stake in a decision it does not control, in a record the county must keep. It is also **oversight** at the smallest scale, because your comment is now in the record the decision must rest on. The specific claim of this chapter is that a comment advances these values only when it adds checkable evidence about the criteria the decision turns on. A comment that reads as sentiment is participation of the kind Arnstein [-@arnstein1969] placed on the lower rungs: heard, counted, and set aside. Write it so it reads well out of context years from now, because that is when someone opening the docket file may need it.
:::

## Commenting at the state and federal levels

The genre works the same way above the county, with longer documents and more formal rules. In Colorado, the State Administrative Procedure Act requires an agency proposing a rule to publish notice in the *Colorado Register* and give the public an opportunity to submit written comments and to be heard. <!-- TODO: verify the notice, comment, and hearing requirements of C.R.S. 24-4-103 and the Colorado Register's current location before teaching from them. --> Check the Register when a technology law you care about passes; rulemaking is where it becomes operational.

At the federal level, the Administrative Procedure Act's rulemaking section, 5 U.S.C. § 553, requires an agency proposing a substantive rule to publish notice in the Federal Register, give the public an opportunity to participate "through submission of written data, views, or arguments," and consider what it receives [@apa_553]. Substantive comments become part of the *administrative record* a court examines if the rule is challenged, and courts look for an agency's engagement with substantive comments, not a response to each copy of a campaign letter [@crs_public_comments]. Agencies treat identical comments as one comment with many signatories, and the Federal Communications Commission's 2017 net neutrality docket showed that floods can be faked: millions of its comments were filed under fabricated or stolen identities. <!-- TODO: cite the New York Attorney General's 2021 report on fake FCC comments if this sentence stays. --> In 2025 the White House directed agencies to lean on the "good cause" exception when repealing rules, which skips comment altogether. <!-- TODO: verify the April 2025 presidential memorandum on repealing regulations without notice and comment, and cite it if the sentence stays. -->

For data scientists, the most useful federal dockets are often Paperwork Reduction Act notices. An agency that wants to start, change, or renew a collection of information from the public (a survey, a reporting form, the required fields of a federal data system) must publish a notice and invite comment, first for 60 days and then for 30 while the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs reviews it. The notices ask the same four questions every time: whether the collection is necessary and has practical utility, whether the burden estimate is accurate, how to enhance the quality, utility, and clarity of the information, and how to minimize the burden on respondents. "Practical utility" is where an erosion audit (@sec-ch-05-erosion-ownership) shows who depends on a dataset an agency proposes to cut; "minimize the burden" is where a refusal specification (@sec-ch-13-critical-data-studies-refusal) argues a field should not be collected at all.

Regulations.gov, the shared federal docket system, has an API through api.data.gov with a free key [@regulationsgov_api]. Document search, docket retrieval, and comment retrieval are separate endpoints; submission uses a separate key, and for a single comment the web form is simpler.

```python
import os

API_KEY = os.environ["REGULATIONS_GOV_API_KEY"]
BASE = "https://api.regulations.gov/v4"
HEADERS = {"X-Api-Key": API_KEY}

def open_documents(keyword, doc_type="Proposed Rule", after="2027-04-01"):
    params = {"filter[searchTerm]": keyword,
              "filter[documentType]": doc_type,      # or "Notice" for PRA notices
              "filter[commentEndDate][ge]": after,    # still open on this date
              "page[size]": 25, "sort": "-postedDate"}
    r = requests.get(f"{BASE}/documents", headers=HEADERS, params=params, timeout=30)
    r.raise_for_status()
    return r.json()["data"]

for h in open_documents("information collection", doc_type="Notice")[:5]:
    a = h["attributes"]
    print(a["docketId"], a.get("commentEndDate"), a["title"][:60])
# (results depend on the day you run it)
```

The API has changed versions and authentication more than once; confirm the current version before writing code. A federal comment has the same shape as a county one, with the Federal Register citation in the header and the body organized by the notice's numbered questions. The Federal Trade Commission's 2022 commercial surveillance docket (FTC-2022-0053) holds good academic comments to read as templates.
